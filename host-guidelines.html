Host guidelines

Last updated 27 November 2026

Hosts make Overhere work. You choose the plan, decide who joins and set the tone for the group. This guide sets out what you're responsible for, what you're entitled to, and how to run a plan people want to come back to. It sits on top of the Community guidelines and the Terms, which apply to you too.

The short version

  • Only post plans you'll go to yourself, at a public venue, with honest details and costs.
  • Choose who joins on what their request says, not on who they are.
  • Keep the chat about the plan, and keep members' details private.
  • Record attendance honestly after the plan, and cancel in the app as early as you can if you have to.

1. Before the plan

  • Only post a plan you'll attend yourself: hosts are part of the plan. Posting needs your two photos and a passed face check.
  • Describe the activity honestly: what it is, where it is and what it costs. The cost is shown as Equal Split, Go Dutch or Host Pays, and plans don't take payment in the app.
  • Give the date, time and venue as accurately as you can. Choose a public place that suits the activity.
  • Set the group size you can host: 2 to 10 people, not counting you.
  • Set how long the plan lasts (1 to 24 hours). The group chat closes 1 hour after that.
  • Use the audience setting as it's meant to be used, such as women-only plans. Don't try to get around it.
  • Don't use the description to advertise, sell, recruit or promote anything else.

2. Reviewing requests

  • Read the person's profile and their note before you decide.
  • Decide on the request, not on religion, caste, gender, sexual orientation, disability or nationality, except where the plan's audience setting applies.
  • Don't accept someone whose request is really for a private meeting or something outside the plan.
  • Don't ask anyone for money, bank details or personal information.

3. During the plan

  • Be at the venue at the time you posted, or tell the group in the chat if you're running late.
  • Keep the group chat about the plan. Be kind and be clear.
  • Don't share a member's personal details, such as their phone number, address or social media, without their permission.
  • Respect members who want to leave early or don't want photos taken.
  • Don't bring or share alcohol or drugs with people who haven't chosen to have them, and don't pressure anyone to drink.

4. Attendance and cancelling

  • After each plan, the app asks you to rate it and record who showed up and who didn't. Record it honestly: it builds everyone's reliability record.
  • People who left the plan in the app beforehand aren't on your list, so they're never marked as no-shows.
  • If you can't go ahead with a plan, cancel it in the app as early as you can. Everyone who joined is told straight away.

5. Your rights as a host

  • Choose who joins. You can accept or decline any request, and you don't have to give a reason.
  • Set the plan. You choose the activity, venue, date, length and group size, within the limits above.
  • Remove a member at any time, with one of these reasons: they asked to be removed; harassment or unwelcome messages; unsafe or inappropriate conduct; repeatedly late; spam or promotion; or another reason you type in. They are told.
  • Get help. If a plan feels unsafe, use the SOS button in the group chat to call 112 or alert your trusted contact. Any member can use it, not just the host. Report the person or message afterwards so our team can act.
  • Have your side heard. If a member disputes the attendance you recorded, our team looks at both sides.
  • Appeal decisions about your account, as set out in the Community guidelines.
  • Be treated with respect. Members who abuse or harass a host can be reported, and the same rules apply to them as to anyone else.

6. Tips for a good plan

Before you post

  • Say what the plan is like: the pace, the skill level and what to bring, so people know what they're asking to join.
  • Post a few days ahead. More time gives people a chance to ask, and gives you time to fill the group.
  • Choose a venue that fits your group and is easy to reach by public transport.
  • Be clear about cost, so nobody gets a surprise.
  • Read each note. A short, honest note usually tells you whether someone is right for the plan.

When the plan starts

  • Confirm in the group chat the day before: time, venue and what to bring.
  • Arrive early and greet people at the venue.
  • Start with a quick round of names and one thing each person is into.

During the plan

  • Check in with quieter members, and ask questions that bring everyone in.
  • Have a backup idea in case the plan doesn't land, such as a different spot or a topic to talk about.
  • Respect the venue: order something, keep the space tidy and thank the staff.
  • Take photos only with consent.

After the plan

  • Record attendance while it's fresh.
  • Thank everyone in the chat and say what you enjoyed. The chat stays open for an hour after the plan ends, which is a good time to suggest doing it again. You can also set a plan to repeat every week, every 2 weeks or every month.
