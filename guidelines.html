Community guidelines

Last updated 27 November 2026

Overhere is for meeting people in your city for a plan you're already making. These guidelines explain how to be a good member, what isn't allowed, and what happens when someone breaks them. They apply to your profile, your plans, your requests, your group chats and everything you send in the app, and to how you behave when you meet. They form part of our Terms. Hosts also follow the Host guidelines.

Our three rules

  • Be real. Use the name you go by, your own photos and a true profile. Only you can use your account.
  • Be respectful. Treat everyone the way you'd want to be treated, online and at the meetup.
  • Be safe. Look after yourself and others, and report anything that doesn't feel right.

1. Who can use Overhere

  • You must be 18 or older.
  • One account per person.
  • Your two photos must be clear, recent photos of you, with no heavy filters. The face check matches your live face against both, and it lasts 6 months before you do it again.

2. How plans work

  • A plan is a single activity, such as a coffee, a film or a game. It isn't a standing group, a community or a trip.
  • Plans meet in person at a venue, for 2 to 10 people plus the host. Don't post plans that are really private, one-to-one meetings.
  • Plans don't take payment in the app. The cost is shown as Equal Split, Go Dutch or Host Pays: be clear and honest about what it will cost.
  • Hosts decide who joins. Asking to join is free, and a host can accept or decline any request.
  • Only people who were accepted should come. Don't bring anyone who isn't on the plan.
  • Only post a plan you intend to go to. Give the date, time and venue as accurately as you can.

3. Posting and requesting

  • Write a plan description that says what the activity is. Don't use it to advertise, sell, recruit or promote anything else.
  • Only ask to join plans you intend to go to. Keep the note you add short, honest and respectful.
  • Use the audience setting (such as women-only plans) as it is meant to be used. Don't try to get around it.

4. At the meetup

  • Show up if you say you will. If you can't come, leave the plan in the app as early as you can, with a reason.
  • Respect everyone's choices, including if someone wants to leave early or doesn't want photos taken.
  • Ask before you take or share photos or video of anyone, and don't share photos or details of others outside the app without asking them first.
  • Don't bring or share alcohol or drugs with people who haven't chosen to have them, and don't pressure anyone to drink or to do anything they don't want to.
  • Be courteous to venue staff. If you agree to split a cost, pay your share.
  • Look after yourself: meet in public places, tell someone you trust where you're going, and use the SOS button in the group chat if you need help.

5. Attendance and reliability

  • Turn up, or leave the plan in time, so the host and the group can plan.
  • After each plan, the host records who showed up and who didn't. People who left the plan in the app beforehand aren't counted as no-shows.
  • Your profile shows how many meetups you've been to and turned up for. Hosts see it when you ask to join.
  • If you're marked as a no-show and think it's wrong, you can reply in the app and give your side; our team looks at disputes.
  • Repeated no-shows can lead to action.

6. Group chats

  • Each plan has one group chat, for the host and accepted members. Keep it about the plan.
  • Be kind and be clear. Don't share anyone's personal details, such as their phone number, address or social media, without their permission.
  • Chats close 1 hour after the plan ends. Nobody can post after that, but the history stays visible.
  • Hosts can remove a member for a listed reason, and members can leave at any time.

7. Keeping contact respectful

  • Don't use plans to move people into private contact outside the app.
  • If someone stops replying, that's an answer. Don't keep messaging them, in the app or outside it.
  • Blocking someone is final. Don't try to reach them through another account.
  • A single clumsy comment can be corrected kindly. A pattern of unwanted attention, messages or comments is a violation, even when each one looks small.

8. What isn't allowed

We don't allow any of the following. The list isn't complete, and we may act on other behaviour that puts people at risk.

  • Child safety: anyone under 18 on Overhere or at a plan, any sexual content involving a child, or any attempt to contact or meet a child.
  • Sexual content and harassment: unwanted sexual messages or advances, sexual content shared without consent, and sexual pressure at a meetup.
  • Threats and violence: threats, intimidation, violence, or encouraging harm to anyone.
  • Harassment and hate: bullying, repeated unwelcome messages, abuse, or hate based on religion, caste, gender, sexual orientation, disability, nationality or any other identity.
  • Impersonation and fake profiles: pretending to be someone else, using someone else's photos, or a profile that isn't you.
  • Ban evasion: making a new account after being removed, or using someone else's account to get back in.
  • Spam and promotion: selling products or services, recruiting for schemes, or promoting other apps, businesses or events in plans or chats.
  • Scams and fraud: asking for money, bank details or personal information, or any financial scheme.
  • Illegal activity: anything the law doesn't allow, including drugs and weapons.
  • Dangerous plans: plans that put people at risk, or ask them to take risks they haven't agreed to.
  • Off-platform pressure: using Overhere to push people into private meetings, or into things outside the app.
  • Self-harm: content that encourages self-harm, suicide or disordered eating. If you're struggling, reach out to someone you trust or to a helpline.

Lines we don't adjust

These lead to permanent removal straight away, without a warning first: anything that puts a child at risk, threats and violence, sexual harassment and intimate images shared without consent, fraud and scams, and ban evasion.

9. Reporting

If something doesn't feel right, report it. You can report a person or a message from inside the app, and block anyone. You can also email admin@overhere.social, for example about a plan.

  • Tell us what happened. We don't tell the person who reported them.
  • Reports are for real violations, not disagreements. A false report is a violation too.
  • Reports about a child, or about sexual content involving a minor, are looked at first.
  • If you're in danger, call 112. The SOS button in the group chat also lets you alert your trusted contact.

10. What happens next, and appeals

Our team looks at reports and at what the app records. We act in three steps:

  • Educational warning. For a first, low-level problem, we explain what went wrong and what to do differently.
  • Formal warning. For a repeat or a more serious problem. A formal warning counts the next time.
  • Permanent removal. When the behaviour continues after a formal warning, or the harm is serious.

The lines we don't adjust skip straight to permanent removal, and ban evasion removes every account involved. We may also remove a message, photo, plan or profile detail, and hide a plan or profile while we look into a report. Where the law and safety allow, we'll tell you when we've acted on a report you made, or on your account.

Appeals. If we give you a formal warning or remove your account, you can appeal by emailing admin@overhere.social. If we uphold the decision, we'll explain why; if the appeal succeeds, we reverse it. A permanent removal is final unless it is overturned on appeal.

We update these guidelines from time to time, and every version is dated. If a change matters to you, we'll tell you in the app first. Questions or reports: admin@overhere.social. Grievance Officer: Kajal Aswani.
