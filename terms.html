By creating an account you agree to these terms and to the Community guidelines, which form part of them. If you host plans, the Host guidelines apply to you too.

The short version

  • You must be 18 or over, and you must be yourself: your real name, date of birth and gender.
  • Be respectful, show up when you say you will, and leave the plan in the app early if you can't make it.

  • Report anyone who makes you uncomfortable. We can remove content and accounts that break these rules.

1. Who we are and what Overhere is

Overhere is a place to find company for things you're already doing in your city. It is not a dating app, a ticketing or booking service, or an events company. We don't organise, attend, supervise or pay for any plan. Plans are between the people who make and join them.

Some hosts and plans are samples, made up so the app never looks empty. They are labelled "Sample". Nobody real is behind them, and nobody will be at those plans, so don't go to meet anyone there.

2. Your account

  • You must be at least 18 years old.
  • Use the name you go by, your real date of birth and your gender, as you enter them at sign-up. Don't pretend to be someone else, and don't choose a gender to get into plans meant for a different group. Date of birth and gender can't be changed in the app; if one is wrong, email us.
  • One account per person. Keep your sign-in details to yourself: you're responsible for what happens on your account.
  • You can sign up and browse plans without photos or a face check. To post a plan or ask to join one you need two clear photos of yourself and a passed face check. A face check lasts 6 months; after that the app asks you to do it again. Don't try to get around it, for example with a photo, a video or someone else's face.
  • How we handle your data is set out in the Privacy policy.

3. Community guidelines

The Community guidelines set out how to behave on Overhere. They apply everywhere on Overhere, including plans, join requests, profiles, group chats, and when you meet in person. In summary:

Be real. Use your own name, your own photos and a true profile.

Be respectful. No harassment, bullying, threats, hate speech, or insults about anyone's religion, caste, race, gender, sexuality, disability, or where they're from.

Not a dating app. No sexual messages, images or advances, and no pressure to meet one-to-one, exchange numbers or go somewhere private. Take "no" the first time.

Be honest. Post plans you actually intend to go to, with the real time, place and costs. No fake profiles, fake plans or misleading details.

Be reliable. Turn up when you've said yes. If you can't, leave the plan in the app, or cancel it if you're the host, as early as you can.

Respect privacy. Don't share other people's messages, photos, phone numbers or details outside the app without their permission, and don't photograph or record people without asking.

Nothing illegal or harmful. No drugs, weapons, gambling, violence, or anything that puts people at risk. Drink responsibly, and never pressure anyone to drink.

No selling or spam. Don't use plans or chats to sell things, recruit, promote businesses or events you profit from, ask for money, or post links to scams.

Adults only. Never bring anyone under 18 into the app or to a plan.

4. Meeting people safely

You'll be meeting people you don't know. Overhere doesn't do background checks, and the face check only confirms that a real person made the account and matches their photos, not who they are or what they're like. Please:

  • Meet in busy public places, and make your own way there and back.
  • Add a trusted contact in your profile. From a plan's group chat you can send them your plan in one tap.
  • Keep personal details, like your home address or workplace, to yourself until you trust someone.
  • Never send money to someone you met on Overhere, or lend them anything valuable.
  • Leave whenever you want: you don't owe anyone an explanation.
  • In an emergency, call 112. The SOS button in a group chat helps you call 112 and send your location to your trusted contact. Any member can use it, not just the host. To tell us about what happened, report the person or message, or email us.

You're responsible for your own safety and decisions, including who you meet and where.

5. Plans, chats, costs and cancelling

  • A plan has a date, a time, a venue, a length (1 to 24 hours) and room for 2 to 10 people, not counting the host.
  • Hosts choose who joins. A host can accept or decline any request, and can remove a member for one of the reasons listed in the Host guidelines.
  • Each plan says how costs work: Equal Split, Go Dutch (everyone pays their own way), or Host Pays. Settle costs between yourselves. Overhere doesn't collect, hold or refund any money.
  • Plans limited to some groups (for example women-only) are shown only to people in that group who have passed the face check. The face check doesn't confirm gender, so hosts decide who they accept.
  • Any member can leave a plan at any time, with a reason. The host is told.
  • Each plan has a group chat for the host and accepted members. It closes 1 hour after the plan ends: you can still read it, but nobody can post.
  • If plans change, hosts should update or cancel them in the app, so members are told.

You own what you write on Overhere: your profile, plans, notes and messages. You let us store it, show it to the people it's meant for, and use it to run and improve the app. You're responsible for it, and you confirm you have the right to post it. Messages you sent in group chats stay after you leave a plan or delete your account, without your name, so the conversation still makes sense for the others.

Don't post anything that belongs to someone else unless you have the right to, such as copied text, lyrics or artwork. To report copyright infringement, see our Copyright policy. We remove infringing content when we get a valid notice, and we close the accounts of repeat infringers.

7. Reports, enforcement and appeals

You can report a person or a message, and block anyone, from inside the app. Reports are confidential: the person isn't told who reported them. Report real violations, not disagreements; a false report is a violation too.

When someone breaks these terms or the Community guidelines, we act in three steps:

  • Educational warning for a first, low-level problem.
  • Formal warning for a repeat or a more serious problem. A formal warning counts the next time.
  • Permanent removal when the behaviour continues after a formal warning, or the harm is serious.

Some behaviour leads to removal straight away, without a warning: anything that puts a child at risk, threats and violence, sexual harassment and intimate images shared without consent, fraud and scams, and making a new account after being removed. We may also remove plans, messages or profile details that break these rules, and hide a plan or profile while we look into a report. We share information with the police or other authorities when the law requires it, or to protect someone from serious harm.

Appeals. If we give you a formal warning or remove your account, you can appeal by emailing admin@overhere.social. Someone who wasn't involved in the original decision looks at your appeal. If we uphold the decision, we'll explain why; if the appeal succeeds, we reverse it.
